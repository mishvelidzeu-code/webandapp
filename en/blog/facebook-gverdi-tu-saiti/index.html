Facebook page or website — which does a business need?

You have a Facebook and Instagram page and wonder whether you need a website. When social media is enough, what your own site adds and how both work together.

Published: 2026-09-12 · 3 min read

Many businesses in Georgia sell successfully through Facebook and Instagram alone — and their question is fair: why do I need a website if customers already message me? This article does not claim everyone needs a website. It helps you work out whether you do, and when.

When a Facebook page is enough

Honestly — it often is. A social page is free, quick to set up, gives direct communication and makes advertising simple. If your customers find you on social media anyway (recommendations, groups, ads) and do not search on Google; if the offer is simple and changes often (daily menu, promotions, new stock); if selling needs personal messaging and you can keep up; if the business is still a hypothesis you are testing — then a website is not today’s priority. Sales first, infrastructure later.

When it is no longer enough

  • Customers search for you on Google. “Dentist in Batumi”, “custom furniture”, “accounting services” — a Facebook page practically never appears for such queries. People searching on Google already intend to buy — and they go to the competitor who has a website.
  • You answer the same questions again and again. Price, address, hours, terms, “do you have X” — all of it is written once on a website and works around the clock.
  • You need trust. Before a serious purchase, a B2B contract or a tender, a company is checked on its website. A Facebook page is like a market stall; a website is like a shop.
  • You depend on an algorithm. The platform decides who sees your post. Organic reach shrinks year by year, and a page can be blocked or restricted — your “business address” can vanish overnight.
  • Ad budget is wasted. Someone arriving from an ad meets a stream of posts, not a concrete offer. A landing page solves that.

What your own website adds

Facebook / InstagramYour own website
Who finds youPeople who already know you or see an adPeople searching on Google — strangers with intent to buy
ControlPlatform rules and algorithmFull — your domain, your structure
InformationA stream of posts, hard to searchOrganised pages: services, prices, FAQ, contact
OrderingBy message, manuallyForm, booking, online payment — automatically
TrustMediumHigh — especially in B2B and expensive services
CostFree + adsOne-off build + domain/hosting annually
MeasurementPlatform statisticsCalls, forms, sources — in your analytics

The difference in one sentence: social media introduces you to people; a website turns those already searching into customers.

How they work together

  • Website — the base. Services, prices, terms, contact, FAQ. For Google and for trust.
  • Social media — communication. News, promotions, behind the scenes, replies. Every post links to a specific page on the site.
  • Ads — to a landing page. Ad traffic goes to a single-offer page with one button and measurable results.
  • WhatsApp/Messenger — from the website too. Those who prefer messaging reach you in one tap.

What it costs and where to start

A website does not have to be expensive to play this role. A single-page site with services, prices, FAQ and a form starts at GEL 250 and is often the right first step; a multi-page business site from GEL 600. Details: how much a website costs and the pricing page.

A practical test before deciding: type into Google three phrases your customer would use to find a service like yours. If competitors’ websites appear and you do not — that is the answer. If you are still unsure, tell us what you do and we will say honestly whether you need a website now.
